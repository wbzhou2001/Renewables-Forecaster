About

This portal forecasts rooftop solar adoption on the Indianapolis distribution grid. The demo runs on synthetic records drawn from the model in the paper, at the scale of the real dataset: about \(1{,}700\) records on \(K = 245\) circuits under \(R = 51\) substations, \(2010\)–\(2024\). Use Upload CSV to replace them with your own records.

Hover or tap the ? next to any setting in the control panel to see what it does. Drag the panel's edge to resize it. Dragging it to the far left hides it and gives the dashboard the full width; drag or click the tab at the left edge to bring it back.

Reading the dashboard

Adoption outlook

The gray line is observed cumulative adoption and the red line the median forecast, with its band shaded. The horizontal line marks the penetration cap and the vertical line the tipping point; the tinted region is the forecast period. The metric switch rescales everything:

  • Penetration: installations \(\div\) the sector's customers.
  • Capacity: installations \(\times\) average system size.
  • Generation: capacity \(\times\, 15.5\%\) capacity factor \(\times\, 8{,}760\,\text{h}\).

The lower chart shows new installations per year: gray bars are observed, red bars the median forecast, and whiskers the band.

Substations

Each whisker is a substation's HPCP interval for new installations in the next window. The dot is the point forecast (mean of the \(M\) simulated futures) and the dark tick the count observed in the last window. Substations are sorted from the highest upper bound to the lowest.

Grid map

Circuits are dots colored by the selected metric, joined to their substation (triangle) on an OpenStreetMap base map. Hover a circuit to see its numbers and highlight its sibling circuits. In the synthetic demo, positions are synthetic: circuits are scattered over Indianapolis and are not real feeder locations. Uploaded topology with lon/lat is drawn where it is.

Calibration check

HPCP (red) is compared with the paper's ablations (gray):

  • Marginal: circuit-only score, \(\mathbf{S} = \mathbf{I}\)
  • Joint: one score over all circuits, \(\mathbf{S} = \mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top\)
  • Bonferroni: Marginal at level \(\alpha / K\)

Points right of the target line reach the target coverage; lower means narrower intervals.

Method

1 · Records → point process

Every installation is an event \((t, k)\) on circuit \(k\), whose adoption rate is

\[\begin{aligned}\lambda_k(t) = \gamma(t)\Big[&\,\mu_k e^{\rho t} + \alpha_{\text{self}} \sum_{t' < t,\; k' = k} \beta e^{-\beta (t - t')} \\ &+ \alpha_{\text{sib}} \sum_{t' < t,\; k' \in \mathcal{S}(k)} \beta e^{-\beta (t - t')}\Big]\end{aligned}\]

Here \(\mu_k e^{\rho t}\) is background adoption, which grows with cost declines and policy. The two sums are peer effects from earlier installations on the same circuit and on sibling circuits \(\mathcal{S}(k)\) under the same substation, a structured version of the interaction matrix \(\mathbf{A}\).

2 · Fit by EM

Maximum likelihood (Eq. \(4\)) via expectation-maximisation. Each event is split between "background" and "triggered by an earlier installation", which gives closed-form updates for \(\mu_k\), \(\rho\), \(\alpha_{\text{self}}\) and \(\alpha_{\text{sib}}\). The decay \(\beta\) is solved exactly, including the boundary term that matters when adoption grows quickly. A weak Gamma prior pulls each circuit's \(\mu_k\) toward its share of customers, so circuits with few installations keep a nonzero rate.

3 · Hierarchical conformal intervals

The model fitted before \(\tau\) simulates \(M\) futures \(\tilde y_j^{(m)}\) for every rolling window \(j\) after \(\tau\), by Ogata thinning. Each window is scored with the sibling-aware non-conformity score (Eq. \(7\)):

\[e_{j,k} = \min_{1 \le m \le M} \big\lVert \mathbf{S}_k \odot \big(y_j - \tilde y_j^{(m)}\big) \big\rVert_\infty, \qquad \mathbf{S} = \mathbf{C}\mathbf{C}^\top\]

The intervals (Eq. \(8\)) are

\[\hat L_k = \min_m \tilde y^{(m)}_{n+1,k} - \hat Q_k, \qquad \hat U_k = \max_m \tilde y^{(m)}_{n+1,k} + \hat Q_k\]

where \(\hat Q_k\) is the (optionally recency-weighted) conformal quantile. Summing them over a substation's circuits gives a valid substation interval. The network total gets its own one-dimensional conformal score.

4 · Long-range scenario

The model is refitted on all records and simulated to \(2050\). The saturation term is \(\gamma(t) = 1\) until the tipping point \(t_{\text{tp}}\); after it, adoption slows with the remaining headroom to the cap:

\[\gamma(t) = \frac{1 - N_t / N_{\text{cap}}}{1 - N_{t_{\text{tp}}} / N_{\text{cap}}}, \qquad t \ge t_{\text{tp}}\]

Each path also draws its growth rate \(\rho\) from its estimated sampling error, anchored to today's rate, so the band widens with the horizon.

Caveats

  • The conformal guarantee covers only the next-window intervals. The \(2050\) band is a model-based Monte Carlo spread under the chosen saturation scenario.
  • Held-out windows overlap in time and span only a few years, so the coverage checks are noisy.
  • With few records per circuit, the per-circuit background rates absorb part of the clustering, so peer effects tend to be under-estimated.
  • OpenStreetMap's main tile server rejects requests from pages opened directly from disk (file://). The map then switches to OpenStreetMap France's Humanitarian tiles. Served over http(s), such as GitHub Pages or a local server, it uses the standard OpenStreetMap tiles. Offline, the map falls back to a schematic view.
